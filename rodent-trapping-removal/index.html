How trapping is planned.

Trapping only works when placements sit on the routes animals are actually using, which is why it follows an inspection rather than replacing one. On a Flats property that usually means along baseboards, behind appliances and near attic access points. On a hillside lot near Beverly Park it more often means along a retaining wall, fence line or the base of a tree with rat activity in the canopy above.

We use snap traps and tamper-resistant bait stations rather than loose bait, since loose rodenticide is restricted for consumer use and is not something we leave accessible to pets or wildlife. Every placement is documented so the follow-up visit checks the same spots rather than starting the search over.

Indoor versus outdoor placements.

Indoor trapping targets confirmed travel routes in the kitchen, garage, attic or crawl space, and placements go where droppings, gnaw marks or grease trails show current activity. Our mouse control service covers the indoor pattern most homeowners describe first, usually a kitchen or pantry issue.

Outdoor placements handle yard-level rat activity, which is common on Beverly Hills lots with mature landscaping. Stations go along hedges, fence lines and near pool equipment pads where rats travel and nest, and our rat control service covers the species-specific approach that outdoor trapping usually pairs with.

What happens during a check visit.

A check visit confirms whether traps have caught anything, whether stations show signs of feeding, and whether the activity pattern has changed since the last visit. If a placement has not seen any activity after a reasonable window, we move it rather than leaving it in a spot that is not working. If activity has picked up somewhere new, that usually means a second entry point we had not caught yet.

  • Traps and stations checked and reset or cleared as needed.
  • New droppings, gnawing or nesting material logged against the original inspection notes.
  • Placements adjusted if the current spot is not seeing activity.
  • A plan for sealing once trapping shows the activity has slowed.

When trapping alone is not enough.

Trapping removes the animals that are already inside or on the property, but it does not stop new ones from finding the same gap. Once a check visit shows the population has dropped, the next step is closing the entry point itself, which is what our rodent exclusion service covers. Skipping that step is the most common reason a homeowner calls back a few months later describing the same problem.

If the trapping visit turns up a dead animal in a wall or attic space rather than an active one, that becomes a separate task since removal and odor control require different handling than a live catch.

Placement planning for larger properties.

A hillside estate near Beverly Park or Trousdale Estates often needs more placements spread across a larger lot, since a single row of stations along one fence line may miss activity coming from a different side of the property. We map the lot during the inspection and plan station spacing around the actual travel routes we find, rather than a fixed number of traps regardless of property size.

A smaller Flats lot or a condominium unit needs a more concentrated placement plan instead, usually focused on a kitchen, a shared wall, or a single confirmed attic entry point. Either way, the number and location of placements comes from what the inspection actually shows, not a standard package applied the same way to every address.

Working around pets, gardeners and regular visitors.

Many Beverly Hills properties have a regular gardening crew, a pool service, or a dog walker who use the yard on a schedule separate from the homeowner. We ask about all of this on the call so outdoor placements avoid areas those visitors or pets regularly use, and so a gardening crew does not accidentally disturb a station mid-service.

When trapping needs to move faster.

Most trapping follows the standard check schedule described above, but some situations call for a faster pace. An animal active inside a bedroom or living space, a strong and worsening odor suggesting a dead rodent nearby, or a business that cannot afford visible activity during operating hours are all reasons to flag urgency. Mention these on the call rather than waiting for a routine follow-up slot, since we prioritize urgent situations against the regular schedule instead of making you wait.

Questions about rodent trapping and removal

How often are traps checked?

It depends on the situation, but active infestations are typically checked every few days at first, then on a weekly schedule as activity drops.

Do you use poison bait?

We use tamper-resistant bait stations rather than loose bait, and placement is planned around pets, children and wildlife access.

How many visits does trapping usually take?

A contained indoor mouse problem often clears in one to two visits. Yard-level rat activity tied to mature landscaping can take longer since the yard itself keeps drawing new animals until entry points are sealed.

What happens to trapped animals?

Trapped animals are removed and disposed of according to standard pest control practice. We do not relocate rodents, since that simply moves the problem to another property.

Can you place traps in a way that is safe for our dog?

Yes, tell us about pets on the call and we plan placements out of reach, using tamper-resistant stations wherever pets have yard or garage access.
