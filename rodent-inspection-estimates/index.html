What an inspection covers.

An inspection is the first step whenever you are not sure what you are dealing with, or you want a clear picture before committing to trapping and sealing work. A technician walks the exterior perimeter, checking the roofline, vent screens, foundation edges and any place a tree limb or utility line touches the house. Inside, we check the attic access, crawl space, garage and any room where you have heard or seen activity.

The goal is to identify the species, the likely entry points, and how active the problem currently is, then explain what a full visit would involve. On a Beverly Hills property this often means separating out whether the activity is coming from the yard, such as a hedge line or mature tree, or from the structure itself, such as an aged gap at the roofline of an older Flats home.

Why an inspection matters before treatment.

Placing traps or stations without knowing the entry point wastes time and money, since new animals keep arriving through the same gap. An inspection also catches situations that are not actually a rodent problem at all, or that involve more than one species, which changes the plan. A property near Beverly Gardens Park with heavy outdoor rat pressure needs a different starting point than a Flats home with an indoor mouse problem in the kitchen.

We also use the inspection to flag anything that needs a specialist outside our scope, such as structural damage that has gone beyond what sealing and repair can fix, though that is uncommon. Most inspections end with a clear list of entry points and a plan that moves straight into our mouse control or rat control service, whichever fits.

When to book an inspection versus calling it urgent.

An inspection makes sense when you want a diagnosis before committing to a larger visit, or when the signs are ambiguous, such as an occasional noise with no droppings found yet. If animals are actively inside living space, chewing near wiring, or a strong odor suggests a dead animal in a wall, that is worth flagging as urgent on the call rather than waiting for a scheduled inspection slot.

Either way, the same technician who inspects can typically move straight into treatment on the same visit if you want to proceed, so booking an inspection does not necessarily mean two separate appointments.

Inspecting hillside estates versus Flats homes.

A hillside estate near Trousdale Estates or Beverly Park usually takes longer to inspect thoroughly, since there is more roofline, more tree canopy and more retaining wall to walk than a typical Flats lot provides. We spend extra time checking where branches touch the house and where a retaining wall creates a hidden travel corridor, since those are the features that most often explain roof rat activity on a larger property.

A Flats home is usually more compact, and the inspection concentrates on the structure itself: sills, chimney flashing, vent screens and utility penetrations that have opened up over decades of settling. Condominium units add a third pattern, where a shared wall or a building-wide utility chase can be the actual source even when the activity only shows up in one unit. We note that possibility during the walk-through rather than assuming the problem starts and ends inside your own space.

What the estimate actually includes.

Once the inspection is done, the estimate breaks down what a full visit would involve step by step, rather than a single lump figure with no explanation behind it. That typically separates trapping and removal, sealing work, and any cleanup or repair the inspection turned up, so you can see which parts are most urgent if you want to phase the work rather than doing everything at once.

Questions about rodent inspection and estimates

How long does an inspection take?

Most inspections take under an hour for a typical Beverly Hills home, longer for larger hillside estates or properties with extensive landscaping to walk.

Can treatment start the same day as the inspection?

Yes, if you want to proceed once we explain what we found, the same visit can move into trapping and sealing rather than requiring a separate appointment.

What if the inspection finds nothing?

That happens, and it is useful information on its own. Sometimes what sounded like rodent activity turns out to be something else, or the activity has already moved on.

Do you inspect detached structures like a guest house or garage?

Yes, every structure on the property gets checked, not just the main house, since a detached garage or guest house can hold its own entry points and nesting.

Is the inspection different for a hillside property versus a Flats home?

The core process is the same, but a hillside property near Trousdale Estates or Beverly Park usually needs more attention to the yard and tree canopy, while a Flats home more often centers on the structure itself.
