Why sealing is the step that actually stops rodents.

Trapping and treatment remove the animals that are already inside, but a house with an open entry point will keep drawing new ones in. Exclusion work closes that gap using hardware cloth, sheet metal and mortar rather than foam alone, since foam does not hold up against sustained rodent gnawing over more than a season. This is the step most homeowners skip when they try to handle a problem themselves, and it is usually why the activity returns.

On Beverly Hills properties the common entry points differ by construction age and site. Older Flats homes tend to have settled gaps at sills, chimney flashing and where additions meet the original structure. Hillside properties near Trousdale Estates and Beverly Park more often have roofline gaps reachable from an overhanging tree limb, along with retaining wall penetrations and irrigation line chases.

Where we look first.

We start at the roofline and work down, since that is the highest-value area to check on a property with mature trees nearby. Vent screens, gable vents and any place a tree limb or power line touches the roof get checked for both existing damage and vulnerability to future gnawing. From there we check garage door seals, utility penetrations where pipes and wiring enter the house, and foundation vents.

A rodent inspection visit typically identifies these points before sealing begins, so you know what is being fixed and why rather than paying for undocumented work.

What sealing materials we use and why.

Foam expands to fill a gap but a rodent can chew straight through it within days, so we do not treat it as a standalone fix. Hardware cloth and sheet metal resist gnawing, and mortar or masonry sealant handles gaps where the surrounding material is brick or stucco. For a garage, a proper door sweep replacement closes the bottom gap that a foam strip alone will not hold against a determined rat.

  • Roofline and vent gaps closed with hardware cloth and sheet metal, not foam alone.
  • Utility penetrations packed with steel wool and sealed with mortar or masonry sealant.
  • Garage door seals replaced rather than patched where the existing seal has failed.
  • Every sealed point photographed and documented for your records.

Sealing on hillside versus Flats properties.

A hillside property with mature tree canopy overhanging the roof needs the tree limb addressed as well as the gap itself, since trimming back branches removes the bridge a rat would otherwise use to get onto the roof again. A Flats property is more likely to need attention at ground level, where an older foundation vent or a gap under a stucco repair has opened up over time. Either way, the goal is the same: close what is open now, and check the rest of the structure for anything close to failing.

Exclusion pairs directly with our rodent trapping and removal service, since sealing an entry point while animals are still active inside can trap them in the house rather than out of it, so the order of operations matters.

How long sealing work holds up.

Properly installed hardware cloth and sheet metal generally hold for years, since they resist the gnawing that defeats foam and plastic patches within a season. The materials themselves are not the usual point of failure. What typically reopens a sealed gap is a change to the property afterward: a new tree limb growing back toward the roofline, a stucco repair that disturbs a previously sealed edge, or a garage door replacement that does not match the original seal quality.

We recommend a periodic check, especially after any exterior work on the house or significant tree growth, so a new vulnerability gets caught before it becomes an active entry point again. This is a quick visit compared with the original exclusion work, since it is confirming existing seals rather than starting the inspection from scratch.

Sealing on condominiums and shared-wall buildings.

A condominium unit in the Golden Triangle or elsewhere in Beverly Hills often shares utility chases and sometimes attic space with neighboring units, which changes what sealing can accomplish on its own. We seal what is inside your unit's control. Where the gap appears to be a building-wide issue, such as a shared roofline or a common utility chase, we document that clearly so you can raise it with building management or an HOA rather than assuming individual sealing alone will resolve it.

Questions about rodent exclusion and entry point sealing

Why not just use spray foam?

Foam expands to fill a gap but rodents chew through it quickly. We use hardware cloth, sheet metal and mortar instead, which hold up against sustained gnawing.

How long does sealing work take?

A typical Flats home takes a few hours for the main entry points. A hillside estate with more roofline and more vulnerable points can take longer.

Do you trim trees as part of the service?

We identify limbs that give rats a bridge to the roof and recommend trimming, but the trimming itself is a homeowner or arborist task, not part of our sealing work.

Will sealing trap animals inside my house?

We sequence work so trapping confirms current activity has cleared before final sealing closes off the main entry points, to avoid trapping an animal indoors.

Does sealing come with any kind of follow-up check?

Yes, we recommend a follow-up visit after sealing to confirm no new gap has opened and that the treated areas remain clear.

Is exclusion available for commercial buildings?

Yes, our commercial rodent control service includes exclusion work for shops, offices and restaurants with the same materials and approach.
