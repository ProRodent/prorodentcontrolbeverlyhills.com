Why commercial properties need a different approach.

A retail storefront, restaurant or office building faces rodent pressure that residential properties usually do not: regular deliveries, shared walls with neighboring businesses, dumpster areas, and foot traffic that residential landscaping simply does not generate. Properties near Rodeo Drive and the Golden Triangle see this pattern most, where dense mixed-use blocks put food service, retail storage and older building stock close together.

Documentation also matters more for a business than a home, since many commercial leases and health inspections expect a record of pest management activity. We keep notes on what was found, where treatment was placed, and what changed between visits, so you have something concrete to show if a health inspector or landlord asks.

Restaurants, retail and delivery areas.

Food service businesses face the most direct pressure, since a kitchen, storage room or loading dock is exactly what draws mice and rats in from a shared alley or delivery corridor. We focus on the areas health inspectors focus on too: dry storage, areas behind large equipment, and any gap where deliveries create repeated openings in an exterior door.

Retail stores without food service still see activity, usually tied to stockroom storage or a shared wall with a restaurant or cafe nearby. Our rodent inspection service is a useful starting point for a retail space where you are not sure whether activity is coming from your own building or a neighboring one.

Office buildings and mixed-use properties.

Office buildings tend to see rodent activity concentrated around break rooms and shared trash storage rather than throughout the building, which makes the inspection more targeted. Mixed-use buildings, common in the Golden Triangle where retail sits below offices or residential units, need attention to shared walls and utility chases, since activity in one unit often traces back to an entry point serving the whole building.

For any commercial property, our rodent trapping and removal service scales to the size of the space, with placement scheduled around business hours so treatment does not interrupt customers or staff.

Health code and lease requirements.

Restaurants and food service businesses in particular face health code expectations around pest management that go beyond what a residential customer typically needs. A documented, recurring visit schedule gives you something concrete to show a health inspector, rather than scrambling to arrange a visit only after a problem has already been flagged. Commercial leases in retail and mixed-use buildings near the Golden Triangle sometimes specify pest management responsibilities as well, and our documentation supports whichever party, tenant or landlord, is responsible for that lease term.

Getting started with a commercial account.

We start any new commercial account with a full inspection of the space, including any shared walls, storage areas and exterior perimeter, so the first visit gives you a clear baseline rather than a guess. From there we recommend a visit frequency based on what the inspection actually found, whether that means monthly monitoring for a low-activity office or a tighter schedule for a restaurant with a known ongoing issue.

Coordinating with property management.

Many commercial spaces near Rodeo Drive and the Golden Triangle sit inside a larger building managed by a property company rather than owned outright by the tenant running the business. In that situation, we coordinate directly with whoever authorizes exterior and common-area work, since a rodent problem tracing back to a shared loading dock or trash enclosure usually needs that party's sign-off before treatment can extend beyond your own leased space.

For a standalone building, coordination is simpler, since there is only one decision-maker to work with. The same principle still applies: we identify who is responsible for which part of the property before treatment begins, so nothing gets delayed by an unclear scope partway through the job. That clarity matters most on multi-tenant properties where a shared trash enclosure or loading dock serves several businesses at once.

Questions about commercial rodent control

Do you provide documentation for health inspections?

Yes, we keep records of what was found and treated at each visit, which businesses can use to show pest management activity to a health inspector or landlord.

Can you schedule visits outside normal business hours?

Yes, we schedule around your operating hours specifically, including early morning or after-close visits for restaurants and retail stores.

Our activity seems to be coming from a neighboring business. Can you still help?

Yes, shared walls and utility chases in mixed-use buildings are a common way activity moves between units, and we can inspect and treat your space while noting where the pressure appears to originate.

Do you offer ongoing monitoring rather than one-time visits?

Yes, recurring monitoring visits are available for businesses that want continuous coverage rather than calling only when a new problem shows up.

Is treatment safe to do while the business is open?

We can plan placements and treatment to avoid customer and staff areas during business hours, or schedule around closed hours if you prefer that instead.
