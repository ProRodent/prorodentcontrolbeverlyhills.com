Why garages and basements draw rodents.

A garage used mainly for storage gives mice undisturbed nesting space in cardboard boxes, stacked furniture and holiday decorations that rarely get moved. A door seal that has worn down over years of use is often the entry point. It is one of the more commonly overlooked gaps on Beverly Hills properties, since the door itself looks fine from the outside even when the bottom seal has failed.

Basements and below-grade rooms, more common on older Flats lots than on hillside builds, add a second pattern: rats and mice following moisture. A below-grade space that has ever taken on water, even briefly, tends to hold humidity that both species are drawn to, and vent openings meant for that space are a frequent entry point if the screens have degraded.

What we check in a garage or basement.

We start with the door seals, since a garage door bottom sweep is the single most common failure point. From there we check any pass-through to the house, utility penetrations for wiring and plumbing, and vent screens if the space is below grade. Stored items get inspected for nesting material, since a box that has not been opened in years is exactly where an active nest tends to go unnoticed.

If the space also holds a parked vehicle, we check the vehicle itself for signs of nesting in the engine bay, which is a separate but related problem covered by our vehicle and car rodent removal service.

Placement and treatment.

Traps and stations go along confirmed runways, typically against walls, behind stored items and near the door seal itself, rather than in the open middle of the space where activity is lowest. We keep everything tamper-resistant and note placements so a follow-up visit checks the same spots rather than starting over.

  • Stored items inspected for nesting material before treatment begins.
  • Placements documented for a consistent follow-up check.

Closing the gap for good.

A garage or basement with an open seal will keep drawing new rodents even after the current ones are trapped, so we recommend pairing this service with our rodent exclusion work once the active animals are cleared. Replacing a worn door sweep and sealing vent screens are usually the two changes that make the biggest difference in a garage or basement specifically.

If chewed wiring or damaged insulation turns up during the visit, that gets documented and referred to our rodent damage repair service rather than left unaddressed.

Attached versus detached garages.

An attached garage shares walls and often a utility chase with the main house, which means an unsealed gap there can lead activity straight into living space rather than staying contained. A detached garage or a separate guest parking structure, more common on larger hillside lots, tends to hold its own separate nest that has no direct route into the house. It still needs the same door seal and vent checks, since it is frequently used for the same kind of long-term storage that draws mice in the first place.

Guest houses and pool houses on larger properties often include their own small storage areas or half-baths, and these get the same inspection as a main garage. A property with several outbuildings sometimes has activity in one structure that has not yet reached the others, so treating each one individually rather than assuming they share a single problem gives a clearer picture of what is actually happening across the property.

Below-grade basements are less common on hillside properties than in the Flats, where older construction sometimes included a partial basement or below-grade utility room. These spaces combine two risk factors at once: the moisture pattern described above and a location that is often checked less frequently than a garage, since most people have less regular reason to go down there.

What a follow-up visit checks.

A follow-up in a garage or basement setting confirms whether placements near the door seal and vent screens have seen activity, and whether any stored items that were flagged during the first visit still show signs of nesting. If the door seal has already been replaced between visits, we check that the new seal is actually closing the full width of the opening, since an improperly installed replacement can leave the same gap it was meant to fix.

Questions about garage and basement rodent control

Is a garage door seal really a common entry point?

Yes, it is one of the most overlooked ones, since the door can look fine while the bottom sweep has worn down enough for a mouse or even a rat to fit under.

Do you move our stored items during the inspection?

We check accessible areas and ask you to move anything we cannot safely access ourselves, since some stored items are fragile or heavy.

Why does our basement keep attracting rodents even after treatment?

If there is an ongoing moisture source, such as a vent letting in humidity or a past water intrusion, that keeps drawing rodents back until the moisture and the entry point are both addressed.

Can you treat a garage without touching the house itself?

Yes, if the garage is detached or if you only want the garage addressed, we can scope the visit to that space specifically.

What if we find chewed wiring in the garage?

We document it and refer it to our rodent damage repair service, since wiring repair is a separate, more specialized task from trapping and sealing.
