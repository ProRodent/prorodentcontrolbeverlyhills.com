Why Rat Activity Rises Around the Holidays in Beverly Hills

Cooler weather, more food waste and quieter travel routes near decorated properties all line up during the same few weeks each year.

Cooler weather pushes rats toward shelter.

As nighttime temperatures drop through November and December, rats that spent the warmer months mostly outdoors start looking for warmer, more sheltered spaces. Attics, garages and wall cavities all offer meaningful protection from the cold compared to open landscaping, and this shift happens every year regardless of any specific holiday. It simply coincides with the holiday season because of the calendar, not because of anything about the holidays themselves at this stage.

Holiday food waste adds a second driver.

Beyond temperature, the volume of food handled in and around a home rises sharply during the holidays, from cooking for gatherings to package deliveries that sometimes include food items left on a porch. Extra trash volume, more frequent outdoor grilling or gatherings, and guests who may not be as careful about sealing food storage all add up to more available food than a typical week produces. Rats are opportunistic, and a temporary increase in accessible food can extend or intensify activity beyond what cooler weather alone would cause.

Decorations and storage create new hiding spots.

Holiday decorations stored in an attic, garage or basement for most of the year get pulled out and moved around during this stretch, which can disturb an area that has otherwise gone unchecked for months. It is not unusual to find nesting material inside a storage box that has not been opened since the previous year, since undisturbed boxes make convenient, quiet nesting spots exactly like the garage storage described elsewhere in relation to vehicles.

Checking decoration boxes outdoors or in the garage before bringing them into the house, rather than after, catches this early and avoids introducing nesting material or an actual animal into a living space by accident during a busy, crowded time of year.

A box untouched since last January is exactly the kind of quiet spot a rat looks for.

Why hillside properties see a particular version of this.

On hillside properties near Trousdale Estates and Beverly Park, outdoor holiday lighting and decorations sometimes involve running new cords or fixtures along rooflines and through hedges, temporarily disturbing the same canopy routes roof rats already use. This disturbance can briefly increase visible activity as rats react to changes along a familiar route, similar to the temporary effect described when trimming hedges disrupts an established path.

It can also, less often, create a small new opportunity if lighting installation leaves a gap or loose fixture near a roofline that was not there before. A quick check of any new installation point after decorations go up is a reasonable precaution.

What a pre-holiday check can catch.

A rodent inspection done before the season ramps up, ideally in October, can catch entry points before the cold-weather shift and food waste increase intersect at their peak. This timing lines up well with the same roofline and vent checks worth doing before Santa Ana wind season, since both point toward the same general period each year.

For a property already showing activity, rat control and rodent exclusion together address both the animals currently present and the entry points letting more in, which matters more during this stretch than at quieter times of year when pressure is lower overall.

Commercial properties during the same stretch.

Restaurants, shops and offices near the Golden Triangle see their own version of this seasonal increase, often more acutely because of higher customer volume, extra deliveries and, for restaurants, a spike in food waste tied to holiday menus and catering. A commercial kitchen that manages daily rodent risk well during a normal month can still see pressure rise during this stretch simply because of the increased volume passing through it. Commercial rodent control accounts for this seasonal bump specifically, rather than treating every month of the year as equivalent.

Retail businesses handling more packages and stockroom inventory during the holiday shopping period face a related but distinct risk. Stockrooms with more boxes and less frequent turnover than usual can become the same kind of undisturbed hiding spot that a garage or storage box becomes at home, just at commercial scale.

Why follow-up matters more during this period.

Because two separate pressures, temperature and food availability, both increase during the same weeks, a single visit is less likely to fully resolve an active problem compared to a quieter time of year when only one driver is at play. Follow-up visits during this stretch specifically check whether the food-waste pressure has eased along with any structural fixes, since one driver easing while the other stays high can produce a confusing partial improvement rather than a clean resolution.

Homeowners who schedule a follow-up visit in January, after the immediate holiday gathering period ends but while cooler weather still keeps rats interested in shelter, often get a clearer picture of the situation. It shows whether the underlying entry points have actually been closed for good, separate from the temporary food-driven spike.

Managing the holidays themselves.

Beyond structural fixes, simple habits reduce the food-related driver specifically: securing trash promptly after gatherings, not leaving food waste in an open bin overnight, and being mindful of package deliveries that include food sitting on a porch for an extended period. None of these steps replace sealing and treatment for an active problem, but they reduce one of the two seasonal pressures driving the overall increase.

Pre-holiday checklist.

  • Schedule an inspection in October, before the seasonal shift accelerates.
  • Check stored decoration boxes for nesting material before bringing them inside.
  • Secure trash and clean up food waste promptly after gatherings.
  • Inspect any new outdoor lighting installation points along the roofline.

Common questions

Does the increase start right at Thanksgiving or earlier?

The cooling-weather shift often starts in late fall before any specific holiday arrives, while the separate food-waste driver intensifies specifically around gatherings from late November on through the new year.

Do rats actually chew holiday decorations?

Yes, decorations stored in cardboard or fabric-lined boxes can become nesting material, and lights or cords are sometimes chewed the same way wiring elsewhere in a house is.

Is this pattern the same for mice as for rats?

Mice show a similar cold-weather shift toward indoor shelter, though the food-waste driver affects both species fairly similarly since neither one is particular about where an easy holiday meal happens to come from.

When does activity typically ease off after the holidays?

Once temperatures start rising again and holiday-related food waste returns to normal levels, typically by late winter, though any entry point used during this stretch stays open until it is sealed.

Should I schedule an inspection before or after decorating?

Before is generally more useful, since it catches entry points ahead of the season's peak and avoids the extra disruption of moving decorations and furniture around a freshly inspected space right after work is done.
