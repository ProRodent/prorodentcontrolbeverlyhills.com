What a rat control visit covers.

Beverly Hills sees both roof rats, which travel above ground through tree canopy and power lines, and Norway rats, which burrow at ground level near foundations, sheds and dense planting. The two need different placements, so the first part of any visit is confirming which one you have and where it is moving. Roof rats are common on hillside lots near Trousdale Estates and Beverly Park, where mature trees and retaining walls give them a route from the yard to the roofline.

Norway rats are more common in the Flats, where dense hedges, ivy beds and irrigation lines along property lines hold cover close to the house. Either way, we walk the full perimeter of the lot, not just the area near the house, since rat burrows and travel routes often start at the property line. Our rodent inspection service is the right starting point if you are not sure which type you are dealing with.

Why rats find Beverly Hills yards attractive.

Mature landscaping is the biggest single factor. Trees overhanging a roofline, dense hedges along a property line, and thick ivy or ground cover all give rats safe travel routes and nesting cover, and Beverly Hills has more of this per lot than a typical newer suburb. Homes near Beverly Gardens Park and the green corridor along Santa Monica Boulevard see more outdoor rat activity along fence lines simply because the landscaping supports it.

Pool equipment pads, compost or citrus trees, and bird feeders all add a reliable food source, and irrigation lines that stay damp through Southern California's dry months give rats a water source close to cover. Our backyard and outdoor rodent control service treats these outdoor conditions directly rather than only the parts of the problem that have moved indoors.

How treatment is placed.

We use tamper-resistant bait stations at ground level and snap traps along confirmed travel routes rather than loose bait, which is restricted for consumer use and unsafe around pets and wildlife anyway. Stations go where the rats are actually traveling, which for a hillside lot often means along a retaining wall or fence line rather than right against the house.

  • Ground-level stations placed along fence lines, hedges and retaining walls.
  • Roofline and tree-limb check on properties with mature canopy overhanging the house.
  • Placements kept away from areas pets and children use.
  • A follow-up visit to confirm activity has dropped and to move stations if needed.

Sealing and long-term prevention.

Removing the current rats does not stop new ones from finding the same route in, so exclusion work matters as much here as it does for mice. We look at roofline gaps, vent screens and any gap where a tree limb or power line gives rats a bridge onto the roof. Trimming branches back from the roofline is a homeowner task we will point out, but sealing the actual entry points is our rodent exclusion service.

If the activity is indoors, in an attic or garage, rather than only in the yard, the approach shifts toward the same trap-and-seal process used for mice, just scaled to a larger animal and typically a larger, more visible entry gap.

Hillside estates versus Flats lots.

A hillside estate near Beverly Park or Trousdale Estates typically has more mature tree canopy, more retaining walls and a steeper lot, all of which give roof rats more routes to work with than a flat, open lot in the Flats provides. On these properties we spend more time at the roofline and in the tree canopy itself, checking for the kind of gnaw marks and grease trails that show a well-used travel route between branches and the house.

A Flats lot tends to be flatter and more uniformly landscaped. Norway rat activity there is more often tied to a specific feature, such as a dense hedge along a shared property line, an ivy bed that has grown thick over years, or a compost area. Once we know which pattern fits your property, the station placement plan follows from that rather than a generic sweep of the yard.

What a typical treatment timeline looks like.

Ground-level Norway rat activity often responds within the first one to two visits, since burrows and stations at the property line are relatively straightforward to service. Roof rat activity tied to mature trees can take longer, not because the treatment itself is more complex but because the yard keeps offering new access as long as branches touch the roofline. We are upfront about this difference on the call so expectations match the actual property rather than a generic timeline.

Questions about rat control

What is the difference between roof rats and Norway rats?

Roof rats climb and travel through trees, power lines and rooflines, while Norway rats burrow at ground level near foundations, sheds and dense planting. Beverly Hills has both, and the placement plan differs for each.

Why do I have rats if my house is well maintained?

Rat activity is often about the yard, not the house. Mature trees, hedges and irrigation give rats cover and water even when the structure itself has no obvious gaps yet.

Is poison bait safe to use around pets?

We use tamper-resistant stations rather than loose bait, and we place them away from areas pets and children use. Tell us about pets on the call so we can plan placement around them.

Do I need to trim my trees?

If branches overhang the roofline, trimming them back removes a bridge rats use to get onto the roof. We will point this out during the inspection even though the trimming itself is a homeowner task.

How long does it take to clear a rat problem?

Ground-level activity often responds within one to two visits. Roof rat problems tied to mature trees can take longer since the yard itself keeps attracting new animals until entry points are sealed.

Do you treat commercial properties with rat activity?

Yes, our commercial rodent control service covers shops, restaurants and offices, which see rat pressure from dumpsters and deliveries rather than landscaping.
